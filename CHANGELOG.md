# Changelog

## Unreleased

### Fixed

- The audit log (`--audit-log`) and the review bundle's `compliance-checklist.md` no longer say "All automated checks passed" while a MAJOR or MINOR finding is open. When findings are open but none is a BLOCKER, the log shows a warning that gives the number of open findings by severity, for example "1 finding is still open (1 MAJOR) and no BLOCKER finding is open", and says that the draft still needs them resolved or reviewed and that substantive lawyer review is still required. The "All automated checks passed" note now appears only when every finding passes, the condition under which the text report says all required disclosures are present. The BLOCKER warning is unchanged. The playground wheel is rebuilt from the fixed source.
- DOCX drafts: a body paragraph with a bold lead-in, such as "**Note:** purchasers bear market, liquidity and technology risks.", no longer opens a risks section. Before, any paragraph under 120 characters with one bold run counted as a heading, so a draft with no risk section could pass the ANNEX_I.I blocker. A paragraph is now a heading only if it has a Heading style or an outline level, set on it or on the style it is based on, or is a short label that is entirely bold or in capitals. A short label has at most 12 words and fewer than 120 characters, or at most 20 words and fewer than 200 characters when it is numbered, so the bold part titles of the Implementing Regulation (EU) 2024/2984 template (Parts B, E and G run to 12 to 17 words) stay headings. A lone dash is not a word, a label does not start with a bullet or dash, and it has no sentence punctuation after a word; abbreviations such as "incl.", "bzgl." or "Corp." do not count as a sentence end. "Entirely bold" means every letter after a leading number, including the text of a hyperlink, so "5." in plain type before a bold "Risk Factors" still makes a heading. A Title-style paragraph names the white paper and opens no section. A style that is based on itself through a loop no longer hangs the loader.
- PDF drafts: a short body line that mentions a section word, such as "risks", no longer opens that section. A line that names a section is a heading if pypdf reports it in a font at least 15 % larger than the body text, or in bold where the body text is not, or if it is shaped like a heading: at most 12 words (20 when numbered, a lone dash not counted), no sentence punctuation or colon at the end, and either in title case or capitals, or numbered as in the Implementing Regulation template ("Part I", "A.1", the summary fields "01" to "10") with a capital after the number. Unless its font marks it or it comes before the first heading, a line numbered "1.", "5.2", "I." or "A." must also be in title case or capitals and follow a heading or a line that ends a sentence or holds only a placeholder or "N/A". Bullet and dash items, numbered list items in sentence case, the line after a lead-in that ends with a colon, a line that a sentence runs into or out of, and wrapped lines of a capitals paragraph are body text of the current section. Any other short line that names a section ends the current section, and the text under it is credited to no section: a heading in sentence case ("Risk factors"), a German heading with a lower-case adjective ("Beschreibung der wesentlichen Risiken"), a title-case line that ends with a colon, a line that ends with a connective such as "and" (it may start a wrapped heading), and a numbered heading after a bullet list, a table or a page header. That section is then reported missing instead of passing the check of the section before on its text. Before, every line under 120 characters that named a section opened it. Limits: a running page header that names a section can still open it on each page, and the first line of a capitals paragraph that names a section can still end the current section.
- DOCX and PDF drafts: heading text no longer counts as section body. A section that holds only its heading is reported missing with 0 words. Required terms must now appear in the body text, so a PDF field "01 Date of notification" answered only "2 March 2026" leaves the notification date for review, because the word "date" sits in the heading. Regime inference still reads heading text, so a draft whose only ART or EMT marker is a heading such as "Reserve of Assets" or "Stabilisation Mechanism" is still read as that regime when no regime is stated.
- A section whose whole text is placeholders is now reported missing with 0 words and the issue "Section holds only placeholder text such as TBD or TBC.". Before, it counted as a thin answer under review. Placeholders are "TBD", "TBC" and "TBA" (also written "t.b.d."), "to be confirmed", "determined", "announced", "advised" or "provided", "[●]", "[•]", "[...]", "[…]", "[insert ...]", "XX" and longer runs of X, and in German drafts "noch offen", "folgt", "wird nachgereicht" and "N.N.", in any case and with any punctuation around them. In the numbered-field layout a field that holds a placeholder is unanswered: it contributes the placeholder instead of "Label: TBD", does not produce the "marked not applicable" note, and a placeholder in the language field is reported on the language check. A part with no answered field and at least one placeholder contributes only its placeholders, so it is reported missing even when its other fields say "N/A". Findings stay per section: a placeholder field in a part with an answered field, or a placeholder next to other text in a section, is not reported. The notification date of `examples/incomplete.json` ("TBD.") is now missing, so the sample report, the demo capture and the README and DETAILS output blocks are regenerated. The playground wheel is rebuilt from the fixed source.
- Study runs (`micar-lint-batch`): PDF and TXT sections use the same heading rule as `micar-lint`, so a body line that names a section no longer opens it. PDF sections are read line by line; before, the PDF text was joined into one line first, so a PDF yielded no sections.
