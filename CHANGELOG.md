### Added

- `ANNEX_I.NOT_APPROVED_STATEMENT` (Art. 6(3) MiCAR) and `ANNEX_III.NOT_APPROVED_STATEMENT` (Art. 51(3) MiCAR) check the English first-page statement that no competent authority has approved the white paper, and fail closed when it is absent. The Title II check also requires the statement to name the offeror, the person seeking admission to trading or the operator of the trading platform (Art. 6(3), second subparagraph). In PDF, DOCX, Markdown and heading-based XHTML drafts, a statement without a heading is routed by its content. It ends with the line or paragraph of its opening sentence, or of the responsibility sentence right after it, and never runs past a heading, with one exception: a label set exactly like the statement's first line (the same bold, capitals or font; in XHTML only one that names no section) is taken in, with the next sentence, where the statement is incomplete and that sentence is short and mentions responsibility. The statement takes whole lines or paragraphs, so text after it in its last line or paragraph, such as a management body's statement written in the same paragraph, stays with it and is missing from its own section; that check then reports Review or Missing, never a false Pass, and an ART draft, which keeps no statement section, drops the text. A statement whose opening words wrap before "approved", as in a narrow column or a statement set a line per paragraph, or break after "crypto-", is found as well. The lines of its responsibility sentence are recognised by their words and stay in it however they are set, so a statement set in bold, capitals or a larger font, or with its sentences in separate paragraphs, is read whole. In DOCX and XHTML, an opening paragraph without a full stop takes in the paragraphs after it only while they carry on the English opening sentence word for word. Where pypdf reads field 02 or I.02 of the summary table and the start of the statement as one line, the line is split at the statement. Field 02 of Table 2 ("Statement in accordance with Article 6(3)") and field I.02 of Table 4, which named no section up to 1.2.1, now open the statement's section. With the LEI rules and the point rules below, Annex I now runs 20 rules, Annex II 20 and Annex III 18. First-page placement is not checked. German drafts are referred to review until the German wording is checked against the Official Journal. The ART ruleset has no such rule, and an ART draft keeps no statement section, because Art. 19 MiCAR prescribes no such statement (Table 3 field I.02 of Implementing Regulation (EU) 2024/2984 holds the Art. 19(4) statement instead). The white paper of an authorised ART issuer is deemed approved (Art. 21(1) MiCAR) and that of a credit institution is approved (Art. 17(1)(a)), but an issuer exempt under Art. 16(2) only notifies it. ### Fixed

- `COMMON.LEI_CHECK_DIGITS`: a malformed or invalid LEI next to a valid one no longer passes. A draft reads LEIs once it has a party section, or a section or the text of its file names "LEI", "LEIs", "legal entity identifier", "legal entity ID" or "Rechtsträgerkennung" (in any case, also split by hyphens, dashes or line ends, and with a decomposed umlaut) or holds an LEI-shaped code with a letter. Then the rule reads all its text: every section, and the whole text of a PDF, DOCX, Markdown, XHTML or HTML file outside its sections, which the loaders now keep for it (headings, DOCX paragraphs in capitals, Word list items, tables, text boxes, headers and footers, PDF lines read as headings, XHTML table cells, definition lists, spans and sections, text under a Markdown sub-heading, text in no section). Each text is read as written and, where markup or a character reference changes it, as a Markdown or HTML renderer shows it (comments, tags, emphasis, code-span and link markup removed, `&nbsp;` and other references replaced by their characters), so a code that `9999&nbsp;SYNT&nbsp;…`, `**…**`, `<!-- -->`, `<br>` or a link splits in a Markdown draft is read. There every LEI-like code counts wherever it stands: a run of 16 to 24 letters and digits of any script that holds a digit or is in ASCII capitals, or a longer one with a digit that a glued "LEI" (up to 28 characters) or a glued word explains ("Kennung9999SYNTH…"); groups joined by one to three spaces, hyphens, dashes (every Unicode dash, the minus sign, the hyphen bullet, the middle dot) or line ends, and by any number of invisible characters (every Unicode format character, such as zero-width spaces, left-to-right and right-to-left marks, bidirectional isolates and invisible operators, and control characters), that together hold at least 16 (with a digit, or all of one length); and two pieces with letters and digits of a code that a narrow table cell wraps from one line to the next, with the next cell's words between them, as pypdf extracts such a PDF table, where one of the two lines names an LEI label. One that is not LEI-shaped as written (16 to 19 or 21 to 24 characters, lower case, a letter where a check digit belongs, a look-alike character such as a Cyrillic or full-width one or a variation selector, glued to "LEI" or a word, split into groups, wrapped in a table cell) now goes to Review with an issue that names it and its section, or says it stands outside the section text. An LEI-shaped code anywhere in the draft has its check digits checked, a 20-digit number included. The reading is one pass over the text, linear in its length. A run of letters only with a lower-case or non-ASCII letter reads as a word ("Aktiengesellschaft"); text the loaders do not extract (DOCX footnotes and comments, PDF images and annotations, XHTML and HTML attributes, CDATA sections in an XHTML or HTML file, which the HTML parser drops, a JSON draft's title), an entity that an XHTML or HTML document declares in its own DTD, which is not expanded, and codes that are no LEI-like code (fewer than 16 or more than 24 characters that no glued word explains, split by full stops or slashes, a table wrap on lines that name no LEI label, as in a PDF party table whose LEI label stands only in its header row) are not seen. A DOCX, PDF, XHTML or HTML file with text a reader cannot see is also read as a reader sees it, without that text. That reading leaves out the following, and nothing else: in a DOCX, text inside a tracked deletion or moved away under tracked changes, and runs hidden with `w:vanish`, set in a font under 1 pt or with characters scaled to under 1 pt wide (`w:w`), or white or nearly white with no background behind them (a highlight other than white, or run or table cell shading whose fill, or for solid shading whose colour, is neither missing nor white; a nested table cell's own shading counts before the cell around it, white or without fill included), each as set on the run, in its character or paragraph style, in its table's style or in the document defaults, and with theme colours taken from the document's theme; the rest joined as Word shows it with the changes accepted, so a code split between a run and a content control, a tracked insertion, a field result or a smart tag reads as one; in a PDF, text drawn in render mode 3 or 7, painted only in white or nearly white (set with `g`, `rg`, `k` or in a device colour space, or in an ICC-based or calibrated space that the resources name), drawn with glyphs under 1 pt high or wide on the page (`Tf`, `Tz` and the matrices), or in optional content (a layer) that the document's default configuration turns off; in XHTML or HTML, `<noscript>`, the content of `<iframe>`, `<canvas>`, `<object>`, `<video>`, `<audio>` and `<embed>`, the parts of an `<svg>` drawing that are not rendered where they stand (`<desc>`, `<title>`, `<metadata>`, `<defs>`, `<symbol>`, `<clipPath>`, `<mask>`, `<pattern>`, `<marker>`, the gradients, `<filter>`, `<style>`, `<script>`) and its elements whose `display` attribute is `none` or whose `visibility` attribute is `hidden` or `collapse` where their `style` attribute sets none, elements that their `style` attribute hides with `display:none`, `visibility:hidden` or `collapse` or `content-visibility:hidden`, and, where neither attribute sets a display, elements with the `hidden` attribute (with `until-found`, their content), those the browser's own style sheet hides (`<area>`, `<base>`, `<basefont>`, `<datalist>`, `<link>`, `<meta>`, `<noembed>`, `<noframes>`, `<param>`, `<rp>`, `<title>`) and a `<dialog>` without `open`, and all but the first `<summary>` of a `<details>` without `open`; the `style` attribute is read as CSS reads it (comments, strings, escapes, invalid declarations dropped, `!important`), except that a value using a function such as `var()` is dropped too, although CSS applies it. A code that is malformed or fails its check digits in either reading goes to Review, so such hidden text no longer completes a code that the document shows malformed, splits it into groups that are no LEI-like code (but see below for a separator under 1 pt) or glues it to other text, wherever the code stands (in a section, a table, a header, a content control, a section that the draft's regime drops). Hidden text that still takes up its room on the page (a DOCX run laid out white or under 1 pt, all hidden PDF text, XHTML or HTML text that `visibility` hides) leaves a gap where it stands, so it cannot join two groups that the page shows apart into one valid code either; text left out of the page (`w:vanish`, deleted or moved-away text, the other XHTML and HTML hiding) leaves none. Text under 1 pt takes next to no room, so the page shows no gap where it stands, but the reading leaves one there all the same: a hidden separator under 1 pt (a DOCX run in a font under 1 pt, PDF glyphs under 1 pt) inside a malformed code can split it into groups that are no LEI-like code, such as a word of letters only beside a group of another length ("hwupkr0mpou8fgxbt394", which the page shows whole, with a "/" in a half-point font after "hwupkr"), and then neither reading names the code and the rule passes, as in 1.2.1. In a PDF the graphics state is saved before every form XObject and restored after it, also where the form's `/Matrix` is not an array of six numbers (a string of six digits included), its `/Resources` are not a dictionary (the form then uses the resources around it) or its `/Subtype` is not `/Form`, so nothing the form's content sets reaches the text after it; and an array that many optional content membership dictionaries share inside their visibility expressions is read once for each depth, so the reading takes linear time there too (8 000 such dictionaries sharing an array of 8 000 groups take a fraction of a second). Where that reading fails for any reason, it is dropped and the file is read as without it, as before. Hidden text that this reading does not tell apart is still read as if shown and can let the rule pass. Among it: in a DOCX, text in a font of 1 pt or more whose characters are 1 pt wide or more, text in the colour of a coloured background (such as black on black shading), text hidden by conditional table formatting, condensed by its character spacing, raised or lowered out of view or in a tiny complex-script size, a run with `w:specVanish`, a `w:t` in the code of a complex field, and text behind an image or a shape or off the page; in a PDF, text in a font of 1 pt or more (also where an `ExtGState` sets it under 1 pt), text under other content (such as a white filled rectangle), outside the clipping path or off the page, transparent text, text in a pattern, white text in another colour space (Lab, separation, DeviceN, indexed), and optional content that only another configuration or the usage settings turn off; in XHTML or HTML, text that a style sheet hides (a `<style>` element or linked CSS, through a class or any other selector), text that other properties hide (`opacity`, `color`, `font-size`, position, clipping, transforms), a `display` or `visibility` given through `var()`, SVG text without fill or with a zero opacity, the options of a `<select>` other than the one it shows, MathML annotations and text that scripts hide; and a group shown with fewer than 16 characters, which is no LEI-like code, so hidden text that completes it leaves no code to name. Text that the reading leaves out although a reader sees it, and a gap that hidden text taking next to no room leaves inside a valid code, can add a Review, which fails safe. Among them: in a DOCX, white text on paragraph or table shading, on shading that conditional table formatting sets, or on a coloured page, image or shape, white text on patterned run shading whose fill is white or automatic but whose pattern colour darkens it, white text in a nested table cell whose own shading sets no fill inside a shaded cell, a run whose paragraph style and character style both set `w:vanish`, which Word shows, a run that its table style hides in only one of the two orders the reading tries, a complex-script run under 1 pt by `w:sz` but not by `w:szCs`, and a run under 1 pt inside a valid code; in a PDF, white text on a coloured background and glyphs under 1 pt wide inside a valid code; in XHTML or HTML, an element that a style sheet shows although the `hidden` attribute, the browser's own style sheet or a closed `<dialog>` hides it, fallback content that a browser shows (an `<object>` whose resource cannot be shown, `<noscript>` or `<canvas>` where scripts are off), and SVG text in `<defs>` that a `<use>` element draws. This reading takes time on files without hidden text too: a 1.6 MB HTML file of styled spans took 1.01 s to lint instead of 0.74 s, a 1.1 MB HTML file 1.42 s instead of 1.26 s and a 615 KB PDF 2.33 s instead of 1.87 s (ADR 0007). The loaders also keep for the rule the text of each section that the draft's regime drops (an ART draft's EMT, crypto-asset and first-page statement sections, an EMT draft's ART and crypto-asset sections, another draft's ART and EMT sections), as the section text reads it, so a code that such a section shows malformed goes to Review whatever the regime, as one that hidden text there completes does (above), and a change in how the regime is inferred cannot make such a draft pass (a code there never counts as found). ADR 0007 and the rule page give these lists with the attributes and operators read, and the false Reviews that fail safe in any draft (an IBAN, a placeholder such as "XXXXXXXXXXXXXXXXXXXX", a long number in groups, a word or heading of 16 to 24 capitals, a valid LEI glued to "LEI"). Before, both LEI rules passed such a draft once it gave one valid LEI. A draft without a well-formed LEI in a section that reads LEIs keeps its Missing or Review status, and `COMMON.LEI_CONSISTENCY` still reads the sections only and leaves such codes to the check-digit rule.
- JSON drafts: a section given as a list or an object is read as its items, or its "key: value" entries, one per line. Before, the loader wrote Python's representation of the value (`['…', '…']`), whose escapes turned a zero-width space or a line end into the letters `\u200b` or `\n`, so a malformed LEI in such a section could pass `COMMON.LEI_CHECK_DIGITS`. Every rule reads the new text of such a section; a section given as a string is read as before.
- XHTML and HTML drafts: a long run of inline elements in one paragraph (a `<span>` per word or per few characters, as PDF conversions write) is read in time linear in its length. ## 1.2.0 - 2026-10-26

From this release the PyPI package and the GitHub Action share one version number. The tag `v1.2.0` marks both, and `v1` moves to it. Earlier releases had two version lines, listed under [Earlier releases](#earlier-releases).

For `@v1` users this is the first Action release since v1.1.1. Besides the changes listed below, it brings the package changes of 0.2.0 and 0.2.1: the corrected paragraph citations for Arts. 6, 19 and 51 MiCAR, numbered-field XHTML read with `--type`, the rewritten iXBRL check, the `micar-whitepaper-linter` command and the corrected documentation links. The lists below record the changes since package 0.2.1.

### Upgrading

- Expect more open findings on the same draft. The two LEI rules are new in every regime, and a section that holds only placeholder text such as TBD is now Missing.
- `COMMON.IXBRL_TAGGING` is a BLOCKER that no draft passes: JSON, DOCX, PDF and Markdown drafts get Missing and XHTML gets Review at best. So `strict: 'true'`, the Action's default, fails every run. Set `strict: 'false'` for a report-only job, as the starter template does.

- `--coverage` in `extra-args` keeps the strict gate and the CI outputs. Under v1.1.1 it exited 0 before the strict check.
- A DOCX draft is linted by the Action. Under v1.1.1 the run stopped because the `docx` extra was missing.
- XHTML in the numbered-field layout of Implementing Regulation (EU) 2024/2984 is now read field by field. It needs `--type other|art|emt` in `extra-args`; without it the run exits 1 with a message.
- Rule IDs are unchanged. Citation text in reports differs from v1.1.1, so stored reports and snapshots change.

### Added

- `--sarif-output`, `--summary-output` and `--annotations-output`: SARIF 2.1.0, a Markdown summary table and GitHub annotation commands for open findings. No draft text; byte-reproducible; no status, severity or exit-code change.
- GitHub Action inputs `summary` (default `true`), `annotations` (default `true`) and `upload-sarif` (default `false`), and output `sarif-file`.
- Rule reference pages in `docs/rules/`, generated by `scripts/build_rule_docs.py`, with synthetic examples that are linted in tests.
- Landing page `docs/index.html`.
- Rules `COMMON.LEI_CHECK_DIGITS` and `COMMON.LEI_CONSISTENCY` (MAJOR, every regime): offline LEI format and ISO/IEC 7064 MOD 97-10 check digits, and agreement between the party sections and the rest of the draft. Both fail closed when no LEI is found. Each regime gains two rules (Annex I 18, Annex II 20, Annex III 16).
- The full synthetic examples (`other-crypto-asset.json`, `art-stablecoin.json`, `emt-token.json`) state a fictional, unregistered LEI with valid check digits.
- Colour tokens for every playground colour, shared with the landing and rule pages.
- Playground finding cards link to their rule page.
- Research notes in `docs/research/` and architecture decision records in `docs/adr/`.

### Changed

- Version 1.2.0 for the package and the Action. `docs/DETAILS.md` marks the Action inputs and the DOCX input that need v1.2.0 and explains the shared version number.
- The Action pins `astral-sh/setup-uv` to v10.3.0 by commit SHA. v1.1.1 used the floating `v5` tag, which runs on Node.js 20, so every run showed GitHub's Node.js 20 deprecation warning. The Action turns off the `setup-uv` cache and runs `uvx --no-cache`, so a moved `v1` tag cannot reuse a stale build, and it no longer saves an empty cache entry in the calling repository.
- All CLI flags are documented in `docs/API.md`.

### Fixed

- Playground: when the XHTML parser (lxml) fails to download, the check reports that the parser could not load, and the next check downloads it again. Before, the check ran without it, showed "No module named 'lxml'", and every later XHTML check in the session failed the same way.
- The playground brand link's accessible name now matches its visible text (axe `label-content-name-mismatch`).
- The GitHub Action installs the `docx` extra, so a DOCX `whitepaper` input is linted. Before, the run stopped with "DOCX support requires the optional 'docx' extra".
- `--coverage` now changes only what is printed. `--strict` and the output files (SARIF, summary, annotations, audit log, remediation, coverage matrix, manifest, review table) still apply. Before, it printed the table and exited 0 before writing them, so `--coverage` in the Action's `extra-args` turned off the strict gate and the CI outputs.
- A JSON draft can no longer set `source_file`; it is reserved like `ixbrl_validated`. Before, a draft could name a local file, which the source anchors then parsed, and change the reported source type and the iXBRL message.
- Directory runs (`--batch-output`): the summary gains `failed_files`, the files that could not be linted, such as numbered-field XHTML, whose regime a directory run cannot take. The CLI prints it, and `--strict` exits 1 when a file failed or no file was linted (an empty directory, or one with no supported draft). Before, such a file was in no count, and `--strict` exited 0 on it and on a run that linted nothing.
- The sample reports, the review bundle in `examples/review-bundle/`, the demo capture and the README and DETAILS output blocks are regenerated for the two LEI rules.
- `docs/sample-review-bundle.md` lists all seven bundle files and describes the manifest and coverage matrix as they are; it said the manifest records the linter version, which it does not.
- Rule pages: the status logic of each rule on the generic checks now says that a section holding only placeholder text, such as TBD or TBC, is missing.
- `docs/API.md` and ADR 0001 now name all four rules whose CI issue messages are replaced by a fixed sentence, the two LEI rules included.
- Rule-page examples drop the reserved metadata keys, as the JSON loader does, so an example cannot rely on a key a real draft cannot set.
- Rule pages: "Present in Release 0.2.1" became "Package version 0.2.1, built from source that may include unreleased changes", since the LEI rules are not in the 0.2.1 release, and the citation note says the citations have not been re-verified since the pages were introduced (2026-10-20) instead of dating each build.
- `docs/DETAILS.md`: the Action's DOCX input is marked as arriving with the next `v1` release, and the `extra-args` example is `--json`, `--lang de`: the released `v1` has no `--type` flag and still lets `--coverage` turn off the strict gate.
- The audit log (`--audit-log`) and the review bundle's `compliance-checklist.md` no longer say "All automated checks passed" while a MAJOR or MINOR finding is open. When findings are open but none is a BLOCKER, the log shows a warning that gives the number of open findings by severity, for example "1 finding is still open (1 MAJOR) and no BLOCKER finding is open", and says that the draft still needs them resolved or reviewed and that substantive lawyer review is still required. The "All automated checks passed" note now appears only when every finding passes, the condition under which the text report says all required disclosures are present. The BLOCKER warning is unchanged. The playground wheel is rebuilt from the fixed source.
- DOCX drafts: a body paragraph with a bold lead-in, such as "**Note:** purchasers bear market, liquidity and technology risks.", no longer opens a risks section. Before, any paragraph under 120 characters with one bold run counted as a heading, so a draft with no risk section could pass the ANNEX_I.I blocker. A paragraph is now a heading only if it has a Heading style or an outline level, set on it or on the style it is based on, or is a label of at most 20 words and fewer than 200 characters that is entirely bold or in capitals. So the part titles of the Implementing Regulation (EU) 2024/2984 template stay headings with or without the part letter: they run to 20 words, and without the letter Part E has 14, Part B 16 and the German Part A 13. A lone dash is not a word, a label does not start with a bullet or dash, and it has no sentence punctuation after a word; abbreviations such as "incl.", "bzgl." or "Corp." do not count as a sentence end. "Entirely bold" means every letter after a leading number, including the text of a hyperlink, so "5." in plain type before a bold "Risk Factors" still makes a heading. A Title-style paragraph names the white paper and opens no section. A heading with a Heading style or an outline level that names no section, such as "Glossary", ends the current section instead of leaving its text to the section before, unless it is a sub-heading: a deeper outline level than the heading that opened the section, a number that extends the number of that heading ("12.1" or "12.1.3" under "12."), or, in the risk section, a heading that names one risk ("Market risk", "Smart contract risk", "Marktrisiko"; the risk warning is not one). The numbered titles and fields of the template keep their own rule: a part title ("Part K") always ends the section, a numbered field of a part ("A.1 Name") stays in its part, and a field of the summary table ends the summary field before it. Any other bold or capitals label that names no section never ends a section, so "12.1 Market risk", "Market risk" or "12.1 Marktrisiko" in bold under a bold "12. Information on risks" stays in the risk section. A heading that names a section keeps the level of the heading that opened the current section only where it is of a lower rank and names one of the sections that heading opened ("Market Risks" under "Risk Factors"). A style that is based on itself through a loop no longer hangs the loader, and a style based on a numbering (list) style no longer stops it with an error. Limits: a sentence of up to 20 words without end punctuation that is entirely bold or in capitals is read as a heading; a bold or capitals label that names no section, such as "Glossary", stays in the section before it, so its text counts for that section; a heading with the same Heading style or outline level as the heading that opened the section, that names no section, is not numbered below it and names no single risk, ends the section, so field labels set in the style of their part title ("Distributed ledger" and "Protocols and standards" as Heading 2 under the Heading 2 "Information on the underlying technology") leave the text of that part in no section, and the part reads as missing; table cells are not read, as before, so answers kept in a table count for no section, and a part whose answers are all in a table reads as missing (before, its title counted as its text).
- PDF drafts: a short body line that mentions a section word, such as "risks", no longer opens that section. Before, every line under 120 characters that named a section opened it. The text visitor of pypdf now gives the font size, weight and height of each line; on a page where its text runs do not add up to the page text, the lines are read without font information. A line is in a heading font if it is at least 15 % larger than the body text, or bold where the body text is not. A heading that wraps is joined into one line before the lines are read: consecutive lines in the same heading font are joined where the second sits one line spacing (at most 1.75 times its font size) below the first, the first does not end a sentence, the two lines together are still a label shaped like a heading (at most 20 words, no sentence punctuation, a capital after any numbering), and the first runs on (a connective such as "or", "the", "their" or "zum", a comma or a hyphen) or fills most of the column. A line fills most of the column if it has at least 60 % of the characters of a full line of body text (the upper quartile of the lengths of the body-font lines), scaled by the body size over its own size. "crypto-" and "assets" make "crypto-assets". So in a draft with no space between paragraphs, a short heading one line spacing above a paragraph or a heading in the same font is not joined with it. A line in a heading font that fills most of the column, and whose next line is in the same font, one line spacing below, starts in lower case, is not numbered or a list item and is not joined to it, is the first line of a paragraph set in that font, such as a bold risk warning: it is body text and opens or ends no section. pypdf misreports the heights of lines laid out with the T* operator, so where the body lines seem more than 2.5 times their size apart, the line spacing is not checked. A line without a heading font is joined with the next line where it runs on ("or", "the", "their", a comma or a hyphen), the next line starts in lower case, and the two make a label that names a section. A numbered line, a list item and a line on another page are never joined to the line before.
- PDF drafts: a line in a heading font that names a section is a heading if it is a label: at most 20 words, no sentence punctuation at the end, not a bullet item, and a capital after any numbering. This holds whatever the lines around it, so a heading followed by a list item in lower case ("a) Market risk") or ending with a colon is a heading. There are two exceptions: the first line of a paragraph set in a heading font (see above) is body text, and a numbered line right after a numbered body line that is also in a heading font (a bold numbered list) ends the current section. A line without a heading font is a heading if it is shaped like one: at most 12 words (20 when numbered, a lone dash not counted), no sentence punctuation or colon at the end, and either in title case or capitals, or numbered as in the template ("Part I", "A.1", the summary fields "01" to "10") with a capital after the number. Unless it comes before the first heading, a line numbered "1.", "5.2", "I." or "A." must also be in title case or capitals and follow a heading or a line that ends a sentence or holds only a placeholder or "N/A". Where the draft sets its headings in a heading font (at least two lines in a heading font name a section), a line in the body font is a heading only if it is numbered as in the template, and a title-case line in the body font ends the current section.
- PDF drafts: without a heading font, bullet and dash items, numbered list items in sentence case, the line after a lead-in that ends with a colon, a line that a sentence runs into or out of (a lower-case list item such as "a)" after it does not count), and wrapped lines of a capitals paragraph are body text of the current section. Any other short line that names a section ends the current section, and the text under it is credited to no section: a heading in sentence case ("Risk factors"), a German heading with a lower-case adjective ("Beschreibung der wesentlichen Risiken"), a title-case line that ends with a colon, a line that ends with a connective such as "and" and is not joined to the next line, and a numbered heading after a bullet list or a table. That section is then reported missing instead of passing the check of the section before on its text; the limits below list where text still joins the section before. A heading that names no section also ends the current section: a template part title ("Part K") always, and a line in a larger heading font (at least 15 % larger than the body text) that is not smaller than the heading that opened the section, unless it is a sub-heading. Sub-headings stay in their section: a line in bold at the body size, a line in a smaller heading font, a line numbered below the heading that opened the section ("12.1" under "12."), and, in the risk section, a line that names one risk ("Market risk", "Marktrisiko"). A numbered field of a part ("A.1 Name") stays in its part, and a field of the summary table ends the summary field before it (see below). A heading that names a section keeps the level of the heading that opened the current section only where it is set smaller and names one of the sections that heading opened. So a large cover title that names a section ("Crypto-Asset White Paper for ...") sets no level for the headings after it, and a "Glossary" in the font of the headings before it still ends a section, as in a DOCX, which skips the Title style. The Part F title "Information about the crypto-assets" now opens the crypto-asset section; the plural in the titles of Parts E and G does not. A running page header or footer, that is a line among the first or last two of its page whose text, numbers aside, is also among the first or last two lines of another page, is body text of the current section and is skipped as the line before or after the lines around it.
- PDF drafts in the layout of the 2024/2984 summary table, where pypdf reads the field number, label and answer as one line ("01 Date of notification 2 February 2026"): a summary field that names no section, such as "02 Statement in accordance with Article 6(3)", ends the section of the summary field before it, so the statements of fields 02 to 07 no longer count as the notification date. The text after the label on a numbered field line is the answer of its section, so the notification date reads "2 February 2026". In every format, "Compliance statement" now names the management statement section, as field 03, "Compliance statement in accordance with Article 6(6)", holds the statement of the management body, so in a PDF of the summary table the answer to field 03 is the management statement. Fields 02 and 04 to 07 name no section, so their statements count for no section. The rest of a field label after the words that name its section, on the field line or on the line after it, counts as its text, such as "in accordance with Article 6(6) of Regulation (EU) 2023/1114" after "03 Compliance statement".
- Limits of the PDF heading rule: without font information, an unnumbered heading of more than 12 words is body text, so its text joins the section before, and a sentence-case heading ends the section, so its section reads as missing. In any font, a heading of more than 20 words is body text, so its text joins the section before. In a draft with a heading font, a heading set in the body font that is not numbered as in the template ends the section, so its section reads as missing. Two headings in the same heading font one line spacing apart (a heading over an empty section in a draft with no space between paragraphs, or any two consecutive lines in that font in a PDF laid out with T*) are read as one heading that opens the sections of both where the first runs on or fills most of the column and the two together are still shaped like a heading. A short line in a heading font that names a section and is followed, one line spacing below, by a lower-case line in the same font that is not joined to it, such as the first line of a bold paragraph after a manual line break, is read as a heading. A bold line at the body size that names no section, such as "Glossary", stays in the section before it, so its text counts for that section. A line in a larger heading font of the same size as the heading that opened the section, that names no section, is not numbered below it and names no single risk, ends the section, so field labels set in the font of their part title leave the text of that part in no section, and the part reads as missing. A lead-in line in a heading font that names a section ("Holders should note these risks:") opens that section. A running header that is on one page only, or not among the first or last two lines of its page, is read like any other line. A heading that names several sections, such as the Part A title (the offeror and admission to trading), credits its text to each of them, as before.
- DOCX and PDF drafts: heading text no longer counts as section body. A section that holds only its heading is reported missing with 0 words. The exception is a PDF sub-heading that names no section and stays in its section, such as "Market Volatility" in a smaller heading font or a bold "12.1 Market risk": it is read as a line of that section, so its words count as its text, while in a DOCX they do not. Required terms must now appear in the body text, so a PDF field "01 Date of notification" answered only "2 March 2026", on the next line or on the field line, leaves the notification date for review, because the word "date" sits in the heading. Regime inference reads the whole extracted text of a PDF or DOCX draft, headings and text in no section included, so a draft whose only ART or EMT marker is a heading such as "Reserve of Assets" or "Stabilisation Mechanism", or an introduction before the first heading, is read as that regime when no regime is stated.
- A section whose whole text is placeholders is now reported missing with 0 words and the issue "Section holds only placeholder text such as TBD or TBC.". Before, it counted as a thin answer under review. Placeholders are "TBD", "TBC" and "TBA" (also written "t.b.d."), "to be confirmed", "determined", "announced", "advised" or "provided", "[●]", "[•]", "[...]", "[…]", "[insert ...]", "XX" and longer runs of X, and in German drafts "noch offen", "folgt", "wird nachgereicht" and "N.N.", in any case and with any punctuation around them. In the numbered-field layout a field that holds a placeholder is unanswered: it contributes the placeholder instead of "Label: TBD", does not produce the "marked not applicable" note, and a placeholder in the language field is reported on the language check. A part with no answered field and at least one placeholder contributes only its placeholders, so it is reported missing even when its other fields say "N/A". Findings stay per section: a placeholder field in a part with an answered field, or a placeholder next to other text in a section, is not reported. The notification date of `examples/incomplete.json` ("TBD.") is now missing, so the sample report, the demo capture and the README and DETAILS output blocks are regenerated. The playground wheel is rebuilt from the fixed source.
- Study runs (`micar-lint-batch`): PDF and TXT sections use the shape rule of `micar-lint` without font information, so a body line that names a section no longer opens it. PDF sections are read line by line; before, the PDF text was joined into one line first, so a PDF yielded no sections. Study runs read no fonts or pages, so for a PDF whose headings are marked by a heading font they can differ from `micar-lint`: a sentence-case heading ("Information about the issuer") ends the section, an unnumbered heading of more than 12 words is body text, and a running page header is read like any other line.
