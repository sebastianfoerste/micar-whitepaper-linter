# Changelog

This file starts with 0.2.2. Earlier releases are recorded as git tags and GitHub release notes. The Python package on PyPI and the GitHub Action are versioned separately: package releases are tagged v0.x (v0.1.0, v0.2.0, v0.2.1), Action releases v1.x, and Action users keep pinning `@v1`.

## 0.2.2 (unreleased)

Citations and statements below were checked against the EUR-Lex texts of Regulation (EU) 2023/1114 (CELEX 32023R1114) and Implementing Regulation (EU) 2024/2984 (CELEX 32024R2984), as mirrored in legalize-dev/legalize-eu.

### Fixed

- `ANNEX_II.D` and `ANNEX_II.I` no longer require redemption "at par" for asset-referenced tokens. At par is the e-money token rule (Art. 49(4) MiCAR). An ART issuer redeems by paying funds equivalent to the market value of the referenced assets or by delivering those assets (Art. 39(2) MiCAR), and `ANNEX_II.I` accepts either basis. `ANNEX_II.D` asks for the permanent right of redemption, its treatment in insolvency and complaints handling (Annex II Part D, points 9, 12 and 13).
- `ANNEX_II.G.VALUATION_AUDIT` cited Art. 36(3) MiCAR, which is operational segregation. It now cites Art. 36(7), (9), (11) and (12). Its label says that only the keywords are checked, and that requiring the word "audit" is an internal interpretation and not an Annex II item: Annex II Part G and Part G of Table 3 of Implementing Regulation (EU) 2024/2984 list no audit, Art. 36(9) requires the audit and Art. 30(2) the publication of the audit report. Whether a missing audit description should stay a BLOCKER is left to lawyer review. The custody label cites Art. 37 MiCAR.
- `ANNEX_II.I` cites Annex II Part C point 8, Part D points 9, 14, 15 and 16, Part G point 3, Art. 39(2) and (3) and Art. 46 MiCAR.
- `ANNEX_II.I` asks for the affirmative wording of either Art. 39(2) basis ("market value of the referenced assets", "by delivering the assets"), so a negated mention such as "Holders cannot choose delivery of the referenced assets" no longer passes. "At par" in an English ART redemption section is referred to review with a note that redemption at par value is the e-money token rule (Art. 49(4) MiCAR).
- German ART checks accept "Rücktausch", which follows German Art. 39 text reproduced in web search results and was not checked against the German Official Journal. Until it is, they still accept "Rücknahme" and "Rückzahlung", the terms used before 0.2.2. `ANNEX_II.I` asks German drafts for "Marktwert"; a German policy that redeems only by delivering the referenced assets cannot be recognised yet and is referred to review with a note saying so.
- Term and pattern matching collapses whitespace, so a required phrase that wraps across lines in a PDF, Markdown or DOCX draft still matches.
- The starter template (`templates/whitepaper-starter`) cites the ART provisions for its `art` type and no longer asks for redemption at par.
- The audit log (`--audit-log`) and the review bundle's `compliance-checklist.md` no longer say "All automated checks passed" while a MAJOR or MINOR finding is open. When findings are open but none is a BLOCKER, the log shows a warning that gives the number of open findings by severity, for example "1 finding is still open (1 MAJOR) and no BLOCKER finding is open", and says that the draft still needs them resolved or reviewed and that substantive lawyer review is still required. The "All automated checks passed" note now appears only when every finding passes, the condition under which the text report says all required disclosures are present. The BLOCKER warning is unchanged. (#32)
- DOCX drafts: a body paragraph with a bold lead-in, such as "**Note:** purchasers bear market, liquidity and technology risks.", no longer opens a risks section. Before, any paragraph under 120 characters with one bold run counted as a heading, so a draft with no risk section could pass the ANNEX_I.I blocker. A paragraph is now a heading only if it has a Heading style or an outline level, set on it or on the style it is based on, or is a label of at most 20 words and fewer than 200 characters that is entirely bold or in capitals. So the part titles of the Implementing Regulation (EU) 2024/2984 template stay headings with or without the part letter: they run to 20 words, and without the letter Part E has 14, Part B 16 and the German Part A 13. A lone dash is not a word, a label does not start with a bullet or dash, and it has no sentence punctuation after a word; abbreviations such as "incl.", "bzgl." or "Corp." do not count as a sentence end. "Entirely bold" means every letter after a leading number, including the text of a hyperlink, so "5." in plain type before a bold "Risk Factors" still makes a heading. A Title-style paragraph names the white paper and opens no section. A heading with a Heading style or an outline level that names no section, such as "Glossary", ends the current section instead of leaving its text to the section before, unless it is a sub-heading: a deeper outline level than the heading that opened the section, a number that extends the number of that heading ("12.1" or "12.1.3" under "12."), or, in the risk section, a heading that names one risk ("Market risk", "Smart contract risk", "Marktrisiko"; the risk warning is not one). The numbered titles and fields of the template keep their own rule: a part title ("Part K") always ends the section, a numbered field of a part ("A.1 Name") stays in its part, and a field of the summary table ends the summary field before it. Any other bold or capitals label that names no section never ends a section, so "12.1 Market risk", "Market risk" or "12.1 Marktrisiko" in bold under a bold "12. Information on risks" stays in the risk section. A heading that names a section keeps the level of the heading that opened the current section only where it is of a lower rank and names one of the sections that heading opened ("Market Risks" under "Risk Factors"). A style that is based on itself through a loop no longer hangs the loader, and a style based on a numbering (list) style no longer stops it with an error. Limits: a sentence of up to 20 words without end punctuation that is entirely bold or in capitals is read as a heading; a bold or capitals label that names no section, such as "Glossary", stays in the section before it, so its text counts for that section; a heading with the same Heading style or outline level as the heading that opened the section, that names no section, is not numbered below it and names no single risk, ends the section, so field labels set in the style of their part title ("Distributed ledger" and "Protocols and standards" as Heading 2 under the Heading 2 "Information on the underlying technology") leave the text of that part in no section, and the part reads as missing; table cells are not read, as before, so answers kept in a table count for no section, and a part whose answers are all in a table reads as missing (before, its title counted as its text). (#34)
- PDF drafts: a short body line that mentions a section word, such as "risks", no longer opens that section. Before, every line under 120 characters that named a section opened it. The text visitor of pypdf now gives the font size, weight and height of each line; on a page where its text runs do not add up to the page text, the lines are read without font information. A line is in a heading font if it is at least 15 % larger than the body text, or bold where the body text is not. A heading that wraps is joined into one line before the lines are read: consecutive lines in the same heading font are joined where the second sits one line spacing (at most 1.75 times its font size) below the first, the first does not end a sentence, the two lines together are still a label shaped like a heading (at most 20 words, no sentence punctuation, a capital after any numbering), and the first runs on (a connective such as "or", "the", "their" or "zum", a comma or a hyphen) or fills most of the column. A line fills most of the column if it has at least 60 % of the characters of a full line of body text (the upper quartile of the lengths of the body-font lines), scaled by the body size over its own size. "crypto-" and "assets" make "crypto-assets". So in a draft with no space between paragraphs, a short heading one line spacing above a paragraph or a heading in the same font is not joined with it. A line in a heading font that fills most of the column, and whose next line is in the same font, one line spacing below, starts in lower case, is not numbered or a list item and is not joined to it, is the first line of a paragraph set in that font, such as a bold risk warning: it is body text and opens or ends no section. pypdf misreports the heights of lines laid out with the T* operator, so where the body lines seem more than 2.5 times their size apart, the line spacing is not checked. A line without a heading font is joined with the next line where it runs on ("or", "the", "their", a comma or a hyphen), the next line starts in lower case, and the two make a label that names a section. A numbered line, a list item and a line on another page are never joined to the line before. (#34)
- PDF drafts: a line in a heading font that names a section is a heading if it is a label: at most 20 words, no sentence punctuation at the end, not a bullet item, and a capital after any numbering. This holds whatever the lines around it, so a heading followed by a list item in lower case ("a) Market risk") or ending with a colon is a heading. There are two exceptions: the first line of a paragraph set in a heading font (see above) is body text, and a numbered line right after a numbered body line that is also in a heading font (a bold numbered list) ends the current section. A line without a heading font is a heading if it is shaped like one: at most 12 words (20 when numbered, a lone dash not counted), no sentence punctuation or colon at the end, and either in title case or capitals, or numbered as in the template ("Part I", "A.1", the summary fields "01" to "10") with a capital after the number. Unless it comes before the first heading, a line numbered "1.", "5.2", "I." or "A." must also be in title case or capitals and follow a heading or a line that ends a sentence or holds only a placeholder or "N/A". Where the draft sets its headings in a heading font (at least two lines in a heading font name a section), a line in the body font is a heading only if it is numbered as in the template, and a title-case line in the body font ends the current section. (#34)
- PDF drafts: without a heading font, bullet and dash items, numbered list items in sentence case, the line after a lead-in that ends with a colon, a line that a sentence runs into or out of (a lower-case list item such as "a)" after it does not count), and wrapped lines of a capitals paragraph are body text of the current section. Any other short line that names a section ends the current section, and the text under it is credited to no section: a heading in sentence case ("Risk factors"), a German heading with a lower-case adjective ("Beschreibung der wesentlichen Risiken"), a title-case line that ends with a colon, a line that ends with a connective such as "and" and is not joined to the next line, and a numbered heading after a bullet list or a table. That section is then reported missing instead of passing the check of the section before on its text; the limits below list where text still joins the section before. A heading that names no section also ends the current section: a template part title ("Part K") always, and a line in a larger heading font (at least 15 % larger than the body text) that is not smaller than the heading that opened the section, unless it is a sub-heading. Sub-headings stay in their section: a line in bold at the body size, a line in a smaller heading font, a line numbered below the heading that opened the section ("12.1" under "12."), and, in the risk section, a line that names one risk ("Market risk", "Marktrisiko"). A numbered field of a part ("A.1 Name") stays in its part, and a field of the summary table ends the summary field before it (see below). A heading that names a section keeps the level of the heading that opened the current section only where it is set smaller and names one of the sections that heading opened. So a large cover title that names a section ("Crypto-Asset White Paper for ...") sets no level for the headings after it, and a "Glossary" in the font of the headings before it still ends a section, as in a DOCX, which skips the Title style. The Part F title "Information about the crypto-assets" now opens the crypto-asset section; the plural in the titles of Parts E and G does not. A running page header or footer, that is a line among the first or last two of its page whose text, numbers aside, is also among the first or last two lines of another page, is body text of the current section and is skipped as the line before or after the lines around it. (#34)
- PDF drafts in the layout of the 2024/2984 summary table, where pypdf reads the field number, label and answer as one line ("01 Date of notification 2 February 2026"): a summary field that names no section, such as "02 Statement in accordance with Article 6(3)", ends the section of the summary field before it, so the statements of fields 02 to 07 no longer count as the notification date. The text after the label on a numbered field line is the answer of its section, so the notification date reads "2 February 2026". In every format, "Compliance statement" now names the management statement section, as field 03, "Compliance statement in accordance with Article 6(6)", holds the statement of the management body, so in a PDF of the summary table the answer to field 03 is the management statement. Fields 02 and 04 to 07 name no section, so their statements count for no section. The rest of a field label after the words that name its section, on the field line or on the line after it, counts as its text, such as "in accordance with Article 6(6) of Regulation (EU) 2023/1114" after "03 Compliance statement". (#34)
- Limits of the PDF heading rule: without font information, an unnumbered heading of more than 12 words is body text, so its text joins the section before, and a sentence-case heading ends the section, so its section reads as missing. In any font, a heading of more than 20 words is body text, so its text joins the section before. In a draft with a heading font, a heading set in the body font that is not numbered as in the template ends the section, so its section reads as missing. Two headings in the same heading font one line spacing apart (a heading over an empty section in a draft with no space between paragraphs, or any two consecutive lines in that font in a PDF laid out with T*) are read as one heading that opens the sections of both where the first runs on or fills most of the column and the two together are still shaped like a heading. A short line in a heading font that names a section and is followed, one line spacing below, by a lower-case line in the same font that is not joined to it, such as the first line of a bold paragraph after a manual line break, is read as a heading. A bold line at the body size that names no section, such as "Glossary", stays in the section before it, so its text counts for that section. A line in a larger heading font of the same size as the heading that opened the section, that names no section, is not numbered below it and names no single risk, ends the section, so field labels set in the font of their part title leave the text of that part in no section, and the part reads as missing. A lead-in line in a heading font that names a section ("Holders should note these risks:") opens that section. A running header that is on one page only, or not among the first or last two lines of its page, is read like any other line. A heading that names several sections, such as the Part A title (the offeror and admission to trading), credits its text to each of them, as before. (#34)
- DOCX and PDF drafts: heading text no longer counts as section body. A section that holds only its heading is reported missing with 0 words. The exception is a PDF sub-heading that names no section and stays in its section, such as "Market Volatility" in a smaller heading font or a bold "12.1 Market risk": it is read as a line of that section, so its words count as its text, while in a DOCX they do not. Required terms must now appear in the body text, so a PDF field "01 Date of notification" answered only "2 March 2026", on the next line or on the field line, leaves the notification date for review, because the word "date" sits in the heading. Regime inference reads the whole extracted text of a PDF or DOCX draft, headings and text in no section included, so a draft whose only ART or EMT marker is a heading such as "Reserve of Assets" or "Stabilisation Mechanism", or an introduction before the first heading, is read as that regime when no regime is stated. (#34)
- A section whose whole text is placeholders is now reported missing with 0 words and the issue "Section holds only placeholder text such as TBD or TBC.". Before, it counted as a thin answer under review. Placeholders are "TBD", "TBC" and "TBA" (also written "t.b.d."), "to be confirmed", "determined", "announced", "advised" or "provided", "[●]", "[•]", "[...]", "[…]", "[insert ...]", "XX" and longer runs of X, and in German drafts "noch offen", "folgt", "wird nachgereicht" and "N.N.", in any case and with any punctuation around them. In the numbered-field layout a field that holds a placeholder is unanswered: it contributes the placeholder instead of "Label: TBD", does not produce the "marked not applicable" note, and a placeholder in the language field is reported on the language check. A part with no answered field and at least one placeholder contributes only its placeholders, so it is reported missing even when its other fields say "N/A". Findings stay per section: a placeholder field in a part with an answered field, or a placeholder next to other text in a section, is not reported. The notification date of `examples/incomplete.json` ("TBD.") is now missing, so the sample report, the demo capture and the README and DETAILS output blocks are regenerated. The playground wheel is rebuilt from the fixed source. (#34)
- Study runs (`micar-lint-batch`): PDF and TXT sections use the shape rule of `micar-lint` without font information, so a body line that names a section no longer opens it. PDF sections are read line by line; before, the PDF text was joined into one line first, so a PDF yielded no sections. Study runs read no fonts or pages, so for a PDF whose headings are marked by a heading font they can differ from `micar-lint`: a sentence-case heading ("Information about the issuer") ends the section, an unnumbered heading of more than 12 words is body text, and a running page header is read like any other line. (#34)

### Added

- `ANNEX_I.NOT_APPROVED_STATEMENT` (Art. 6(3) MiCAR) and `ANNEX_III.NOT_APPROVED_STATEMENT` (Art. 51(3) MiCAR) check the English first-page statement that no competent authority has approved the white paper, and fail closed when it is absent. The Title II check also requires the statement to name the offeror, the person seeking admission to trading or the operator of the trading platform (Art. 6(3), second subparagraph). A statement without a heading is routed by its content. First-page placement is not checked. German drafts are referred to review until the German wording is checked against the Official Journal. The ART ruleset has no such rule because Art. 19 MiCAR prescribes no such statement (Table 3 field I.02 of Implementing Regulation (EU) 2024/2984 holds the Art. 19(4) warning instead). The white paper of an authorised ART issuer is deemed approved (Art. 21(1) MiCAR) and that of a credit institution is approved (Art. 17(1)(a)), but an issuer exempt under Art. 16(2) only notifies it.
- `studies/2026-07-title-ii-annex-i-whitepaper-study/scripts/build_review_matrix.py` builds the human-review sheets from the detector output.
- Text reports print each citation in English EU drafting style, for example "Art. 6(7) MiCAR", followed by a link to the cited act on EUR-Lex. `--citation-style de` prints the German form the rules keep, for example "Art. 6 Abs. 7 MiCAR". JSON output carries both (`citation`, `citation_en`) and the links (`sources`). (#27)
- Community files: issue forms for bugs, false positives and rule proposals, a pull request template, `CITATION.cff` and a code of conduct. (#27)
